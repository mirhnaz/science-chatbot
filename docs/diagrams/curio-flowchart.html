How Curio works

Curio is a science explorer for kids: ask a question, an AI tutor explains it simply, and three new questions keep you exploring. Follow one question from your screen to our home computer and back, in about three seconds.

Using the app

From a spark to a stamp

Questions link together into a trail. Finish a trail and you earn a stamp for your collection.

  1. Pick a spark

    Home shows starter questions called sparks.

  2. Follow the trail

    Five steps. Each answer offers three dive-deeper questions.

  3. Finish

    After step 5, see what you learned and share it with a grown-up.

  4. Earn a stamp

    One stamp per trail, in the topic you explored, like Space or Animals.

  5. Collect them all

    12 kinds to find. Sparks point to new topics.

The parts

What each part does

On your screen

The app

A website for any computer or phone, and an app for iPad and iPhone. It shows the questions, answers and stamps.

Like the front desk.

Sparks

66 starter questions we wrote, across 11 science topics. Picking them needs no AI.

Like a deck of question cards.

Your saved stuff

Your first name, stamps and unfinished trails are saved only on your own device.

Like a notebook that stays in your bag.

Talking and listening

On iPad and iPhone you can say your question; the device turns it into words itself. Answers can be read aloud.

Like a helper who writes down what you say.

Backup brain

If the home computer can’t be reached, the iPad and iPhone app can use a smaller AI on the device.

Like a spare tyre: simpler, but it keeps you going.

Pixel animation

The moving solar system, atom and stars are drawn by the device’s graphics chip, so the app stays smooth.

Across the internet

The secure tunnel

A service called Tailscale lets the app reach our home computer from anywhere, with everything scrambled (encrypted) on the way.

Like a locked pipe from your screen to our house.

At our home computer

Curio server

Our program that receives questions, checks them, adds the rulebook, and checks the AI’s reply. Written in a language called Rust.

Like a traffic controller and a gatekeeper.

The rulebook

The tutor instructions we wrote: explain for kids, simple and exciting, around 100 words, safe ideas only, three new questions.

Like a teacher’s lesson plan.

Ollama and Gemma

Gemma 4 is the AI model, made by Google; Ollama is the program that runs it. Models can be swapped.

Like a brain and its body.

Graphics card

A powerful chip made for games that does the AI’s maths very fast. That’s why answers take only a few seconds.

Like an engine.
